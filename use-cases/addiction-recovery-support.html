Recovery, remembered

A mind that remembers your recovery without judgment — your patterns, your triggers, your wins — and is there in the hard hours.

The problem

Recovery is cumulative. You build self-knowledge over time: your triggers, your patterns, what helps and what doesn't. But AI tools reset. You re-explain your history and where you are, every time. For something this personal, having to retell it is its own kind of harm. It makes your progress feel unreal, because the system can't see it.

What a synthetic does differently

  • Remembers your whole journey — milestones, setbacks, patterns, wins
  • Knows your triggers, and never makes you re-explain them
  • Setbacks don't erase what came before — it remembers the progress too
  • Connects patterns across time — "this is like what you noticed last month"
  • Remembers what works for you, and what doesn't
  • Your recovery history never sits on someone else's server. It all stays on your machine: no cloud storage, no one else's database.

Because it's one mind

Recovery happens in the whole of a life — work, family, sleep, the friend who calls at the right time. A synthetic that knows all of that, not just your sobriety count, can see the risky week coming because it knows what your weeks look like.

What it isn't. It isn't a counselor, a sponsor or treatment, and it doesn't replace them. If you're in danger, contact emergency services or a crisis line.

One synthetic. Many roles.

Not a separate app for every need. One mind that remembers all of it, together, on your own machine.
