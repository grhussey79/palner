Through grief

A mind that sits with you through loss — remembers the person you lost, holds the stories you told about them, and doesn't try to fix you.

The problem

Grief doesn't keep a schedule. It comes in waves — at 2 AM, at the grocery store, on a Tuesday eight months in. It needs a witness: someone who knows the whole story and remembers the person. But AI tools don't remember. Every conversation starts with "tell me about your loss," and you do the work of re-introducing your grief every time. It doesn't need to be fixed. It needs to be remembered.

What a synthetic does differently

  • Remembers the person you lost — their name, their stories, who they were to you
  • Holds every story you've told, and never asks you to repeat one
  • Connects memories across time — "you mentioned she used to sing that song…"
  • Doesn't push you to be further along than you are
  • Remembers the hard days — anniversaries, birthdays — and can be there for them
  • It all stays on your machine: no cloud storage, no one else's database.

Because it's one mind

Grief isn't a separate part of you. A synthetic that also knows your kids, your work and your jokes knows why a particular song in a particular store undid you — because it knew the person through you, not through an intake form.

What it isn't. It isn't a grief counselor and doesn't replace the people around you. If the weight gets to be too much, reach out to someone you trust, a counselor, or a crisis line.

One synthetic. Many roles.

Not a separate app for every need. One mind that remembers all of it, together, on your own machine.
